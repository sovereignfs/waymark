# Changelog

All notable changes to Waymark are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

Each release's section is also its GitHub release notes: the release workflow reads this file when a release is cut. Write the notes under
`## [Unreleased]` as you go; a release turns them into the version's section.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The repository's conventions: licence, contributing guide, pull request template, dependency updates, and the package that the app is built in.
- Settings, all named `WAYMARK_…`, checked when the server starts: a wrong or missing one stops it, naming every problem and no value. `WAYMARK_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is required.
- The database: sqld (the default), a SQLite file or PostgreSQL, chosen with `WAYMARK_DB_DIALECT`, provisioned and migrated when the server starts; it waits for a database that is still starting and stops, saying why, if it cannot use it. `/api/health` answers for it.
- Every page is sent with a strict Content-Security-Policy (a nonce for each request, nothing third-party, no inline script), and every response with the usual security headers; the app may ask where the device is, and nothing else about it.
- The app's frame: a header with the wordmark, a sidebar (Home, Check-ins, Map, Places) that is full or a rail and remembered on the device, and on a phone a footer and a navigation drawer, in light and dark. Each place is an empty page for now.
- Background jobs run in the web process (`WAYMARK_WORKER=off` turns them off in all but one process that shares a database) and stop when the server is told to; a job's work is claimed in the database, so two processes never do it twice.
- Waymark installs as an app, and a service worker keeps its own files and nothing of anyone's: no page, no answer, no place or note is ever in its cache. With no connection a page says it needs one.
- One image for all three database forms, with `/data` a volume in each of the three Compose setups (`deploy/`).
- Signing in with an email and password, when `WAYMARK_AUTH_LOCAL=1`: the first account is made on the sign-up page, and `WAYMARK_AUTH_SIGNUP=closed` (the default) refuses every later one; `open` lets anyone make one. With local sign-in off the sign-in pages and routes do not exist. With `WAYMARK_SMTP_HOST` set an address is confirmed by a link and a forgotten password is reset by one.
- Passkeys and an authenticator app are supported by the sign-in; setting them up is in Settings, which is to come.
- Sealed rows: every field that says where or what (a place's name and point, a note, a trip's name, a person's name and address) is written into one encrypted envelope per row, under `WAYMARK_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, bound to its table and row, so that a copy of the database holds none of it.
- Every account has a profile of its own: its name and address are kept by Waymark, sealed, from the day the account is made, and are what the app shows of a person; an address is seen only by its owner and, once there are trips, by a trip's Organizers.
- The front address is a short page about Waymark for a visitor, with a way to sign in (and to sign up, where the server takes sign-ups), and Home for someone signed in; Home's own address, `/home`, goes to `/`.
- Photos and files: an upload is judged by its bytes (an HTML page named `.jpg` is refused), an image is turned upright and re-encoded with its location and every other piece of metadata removed, and every file is sealed on disk under `/data/media`, served only to its owner. `WAYMARK_DATA_DIR` (default `/data`) and `WAYMARK_MEDIA_MAX_ACCOUNT_MB` (default 500) are new settings, and `/api/health` now needs the data directory to be writable.
- Deleting an account removes everything of it in one routine that can be run again: its files from disk and then their rows, its profile and counters, and its sign-in. It asks for the password (or, for an account that has none, a sign-in made in the last ten minutes) and says how many files go.
- Settings, from the avatar menu: your name, units (kilometres or miles) and the first day of the week; your password, authenticator app, passkeys and the devices you are signed in on; how much of your room your files use, and deleting your account. It needs a connection and says so when there is none.
- A point's time zone is found on the server and the device with no network, and the local date, time and offset of a moment in a zone are worked out in one place, so a check-in at 23:59 is on the right day on either side of the date line.
- Places: a person's own places, sealed like everything that says where, with the time zone found from the point and the country from the address; they can be renamed, given another category, moved, merged into another (their check-ins move with them) and deleted when nothing points at them, and no one else can see or change them. "The same place" is one rule: the same source and id, or the same name within 50 metres. They are removed with the account.
- Finding a place: the search asks the person's own places first and then the operator's place search, from the server and never from the browser (`WAYMARK_PLACES_PROVIDER` is `nominatim`, `photon` or `off`, and `WAYMARK_PLACES_URL` an address of your own). The public instance's usage policy is kept: one request a second from the whole server, however many processes there are, results kept for a day, and a User-Agent naming Waymark and its address; an address of your own lifts that. With the search off or unreachable the app says so, and a place can still be added by hand. `pnpm places:fake` is a search with a few places that reaches nothing, for trying it out.
- The map's tile service: a style (light and dark, in the design system's colours), tiles and letters, all served by Waymark to signed-in people, so the browser asks no one else. `WAYMARK_MAP_SOURCE` is `proxy` (tiles fetched once from OpenFreeMap by default, or from a tile server of your own, and kept on disk under `WAYMARK_MAP_CACHE_MAX_MB`, the ones read longest ago going first), `pmtiles` (read from a file, with no request to anyone) or `off` (no map, every map address answers 404). Every map shows its attribution. See "The map" in the README for what OpenFreeMap's terms do and do not say.
- Check-ins are written the one way: what a person does (check in at a place, at a new place or at a point named later; change one; delete one; make, change and delete a place) becomes a mutation by a pure function, and one module of rules, run by the browser before it queues a change and by the server before it applies it, refuses what is not a check-in with a sentence that says what to do: a moment in the future, a note over 2,000 characters, more than 20 companions, a time zone that does not exist. A check-in is in the zone the device is in when made live and the place's when made for another time, so one at 23:59 is on the right day.
- The server applies what a device sends once, in order and sealed: each change takes its number in the person's stream, a mutation that arrives again writes nothing, a change that is wrong keeps the whole mutation out (and can be sent again once mended), another person's check-ins and places cannot be touched, and a place with check-ins is not deleted. A check-in made from a point alone, with no network to ask what is there, is named from where it is once it arrives, unless the person has named it already.
- A check-in has up to ten photos, in order, each named by an id the device made, so a photo taken with no network can be sent before or after its check-in and shows a placeholder until its file arrives. A photo that leaves a check-in, or goes with it when the check-in is deleted, has its file and thumbnail removed; a photo uploaded and never attached to a check-in is removed after thirty days. No one else can read a photo, or attach one of yours.
- The sync endpoint, `/api/sync`: a device sends what it recorded with no network, in order, and a cursor; the server applies each once (a retry after a lost answer applies nothing twice), says what became of each, with a sentence for what was refused, and returns what changed since the cursor, in order, a page at a time, with deleted rows as bare tombstones. A device on a protocol the server does not speak is told to update, and keeps what it recorded. No one's rows appear to anyone else.
- Waymark keeps what you have on the device and works with no network: every change is shown at once and sent when there is a connection, in order, once, from one tab at a time, and a change the server refuses is kept with the reason instead of being lost. Two browsers of one account, one of them offline, end with the same check-ins. Signing out sends what it can, asks before it throws away what it could not send, and then deletes this account's copy from the browser.
- Couldn't sync: a change the server would not take is kept, with the reason in a sentence, in Settings under This device, and the avatar menu says how many there are. You send it again or discard it, and nothing is thrown away until you say so.
- A photo taken with no network is kept on the device, shown from it, and uploaded when there is a connection, before or after its check-in, retried with a growing wait if the connection is poor, and at once when it comes back. One the server will not take (too large, not an image) appears in Couldn't sync with the reason and stays on the device until you send it again or discard it.
- Figures: how many check-ins, this year and each year, how many places, countries, regions and cities, and for one place how many times, the first and the last and which trips, all counted from your check-ins and never stored, so an imported decade gives the figures a lived one would. A year is the year it was where you were, so a check-in at 00:30 on New Year's Day is in the new year. There are no streaks, badges or points.
- The record of what was deleted, and of which changes the server has applied, is kept for ninety days and then pruned. A browser that has been away longer than that sends what it recorded and is then given everything again, so it cannot keep a check-in that was deleted while it was gone, and nothing it recorded is lost.
- Check in is the first thing in the sidebar, always one press away, and so is the C key (anywhere you are not typing). It opens a sheet over whatever page you are on, in the address, so a link opens it and the back button closes it; on a phone it is a drawer. Its content is still to come.
