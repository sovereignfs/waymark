# Epic 9: Trips

## Status

📋 Planned.

## Overview

Phase 2's data: trips of stops, days and items, with trip places, attachments and links; planning until ready; and the auto-link that drops check-ins
into the trip their date and place match. Trips, stops, ready, attachments and links are online operations (`/api/ops`); items and day notes sync offline
(6.6). Roles beyond the creator's are epic 10.

#### ✅ 9.1 — Trips, members and access

**Goal:** a person makes a trip, is its Organizer, and nobody else can reach it. **Design:** §5.2, §10.1, §10.8. **Depends on:** 2.7, 4.1.

**Technical:** `trip` (name and description sealed) and `trip_member` (the creator as Organizer, going); `/api/ops` as Tusk's and Tally's (session,

#### ✅ 9.2 — Stops, days and trip places

**Goal:** the shape of a trip. **Design:** §7.1, §10.3, §10.4. **Depends on:** 9.1, 4.4.

**Technical:** `stop` and `day`; trip places (`place.trip_id`); add a stop from a found place, re-date, reorder, remove; no overlap except a shared
boundary day; 90 days a stop, 365 a trip; days made and removed with the dates, a shrink over a day with items refused naming the days, with operations to
move those items or delete them first; the trip's dates recomputed in the same transaction; day title and notes as mutations (6.6).

**Accepts when:** overlapping stops are refused and a shared boundary is accepted; shrinking a stop over an item is refused, and accepted after moving

**Done as:** **Tables:** `stop` and `day` on both dialects (migration `0017_stops_and_days`; a stop's note and a day's title and notes are sealed, `SEALED.stop` and `SEALED.day` were declared and `stop` and `day` join the key rotation with a `version` guard); **trip places** are `place` rows with a `trip_id` and no `account_id`, made with their stop. **`src/trips/dates.ts`** (pure, no zone: dates are days of the calendar and are counted on its own arithmetic, so daylight saving and the date line cannot move one): a stop has both its dates or neither, is at most 90 days, a trip at most 365 days (each stop's days counted), stops do not overlap except for a shared boundary day (which is two days, one in each stop) and a date is in at most two stops; the trip's dates are the first arrival and the last departure; days are reconciled against the dates (made, removed, kept with their ids). **`src/trips/plan.ts`:** `readPlan` (every member, Followers included), `addStop`, `updateStop`, `moveStop`, `removeStop`. **A change is one transaction that begins with a guarded `UPDATE trip … WHERE id = ? AND <Organizer or Traveler>`**, which shows the caller may _and takes the trip's row_, so that what is read after it cannot change under it: two changes at the same moment are done one after the other (tested: of two overlapping stops made at once, one is made), the trip's counter advances once, what the change writes is stamped with it, **a refused change keeps nothing (the counter included)**, and **the trip's dates are recomputed from its stops in the same transaction**. A stop adds a place of the trip's own from the found place's fields (validated by the same function as a person's place, zone from its point); **shrinking a stop over a day that has items is refused, naming the days** (`items`, with `days`), and goes through once they are moved or deleted (items are 9.3: `DAY_REFERENCES` in `tables.ts` is where they are named, and the test uses a table of its own); removing a stop removes its days, what hangs on them, and its place unless something else of the trip names it (`PLACE_USERS`), and clears a cover that was that place; **a dated stop is in the order of its dates, so only an undated one is moved** (`stop.move`, renumbering the undated ones; a dated one is refused and told to change its dates). `trip.update` takes `coverPlaceId`, which must be one of the trip's own places (a person's place or another trip's is not found). **Operations:** `trip.plan` (members), `stop.add`, `stop.update`, `stop.move`, `stop.remove` (`editor` in `OPERATIONS`: Organizers and Travelers; a Follower and a stranger are told it is not there), answering with the plan or `invalid`, `limit`, `overlap` or `items` and a sentence that repeats nothing typed. `WAYMARK_LIMIT_TRIP_STOPS` is now used; deleting a trip removes its stops, days and places (`tables.ts`), and its preview counts the stops. **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1238 tests, on each): the date rules (12), every operation as a stranger and as a Traveler and a Follower, the sealed fields, days made and removed and kept, the boundary day, every limit, the counter, concurrent changes, the refused shrink naming its days and changing nothing, ordering, removal and cascade, cover, deletion, and **a run of 40 random changes after each of which the trip's dates equal its first arrival and last departure, every stop's days are exactly its dates and there is a place for each stop**; with the items refusal taken out one test fails, and with the editor condition taken out twelve. **Left out:** nothing writes a day's title or notes (6.6, offline), and there are no items (9.3), so the refusal over items is exercised against a table the test makes; `/api/places` does not yet search a trip's own places (§7.2, with the plan's screen); a stop's place cannot be changed (remove the stop and add another); no stop or day is on the device or in the export (6.6, 15.3), and no screen draws the plan (11.x).
it; the trip's dates always equal its first and last stop's.

#### ✅ 9.3 — Items

**Goal:** things to do on a day. **Design:** §10.5. **Depends on:** 9.2.

**Technical:** `item` (title and notes sealed), at least a place or a title; kind; start and end in the stop's zone; fixed only with a start; fractional
order; create, edit, move within and between days, delete, as actions and mutations in the trip's stream (applied by 6.6; online until then).

**Accepts when:** an item without place or title is refused; fixed without a time is refused; moving an item to another stop's day keeps its fields.

**Done as:** **`item`** on both dialects (migration `0018_items`; its columns are `start_time` and `end_time`, since `start` and `end` are reserved in SQL, and `DESIGN.md` §24.3 says so): the kind, the times and whether it is fixed are plain, **the title and the notes are sealed** (`SEALED.item`, whose title is now optional: an item has a place _or_ a title) and it joins the key rotation with a `version` guard; `created_by` and `updated_by` name the accounts, and **both are cleared when an account is deleted while the item stays, as the trip's**. **`src/trips/item-format.ts`** (pure, run by the server and later by the device): a place or a title, at least one; a kind of six; times `HH:mm`, an end needs a start and is after it; **fixed needs a start**; notes up to 2,000. **`src/position.ts`** is copied from Tusk's (`fractional-indexing` 3.4.0, exact-pinned; its tests rewritten for the pure part, since its database tests are Tusk's schema), and an item's `position` is the day's order as dragged, a key between its neighbours, compared as bytes (`COLLATE "C"` on PostgreSQL: a test reads the order back from the database). **`src/trips/plan.ts`:** `addItem` (on any day of the trip, a new place of the trip's own from its fields or one the trip has by id, at the end or just before another), `updateItem` (the fields given change, **the whole is checked as it will be**, so no change can leave an item with neither place nor title or a fixed one with no start), `moveItem` (within a day or to another day, of the same stop or another, with **every field kept**; into a full day it is refused), `deleteItem`, and the two ways out of a refused shrink, `moveDayItems` (a day's items to the end of another, in their order) and `clearDayItems`. A place nothing names any more (`PLACE_USERS`: a stop, an item) is removed by one sweep after anything that can leave one, and a cover that was it is cleared. **The shrink refusal of 9.2 is now real**: `DAY_REFERENCES` names `item`, so shrinking a stop over a day with items is refused naming the days, a stop removed takes its items, and the places they alone named. The plan (`trip.plan`) carries each day's items in order. **Operations** (`item.add`, `item.update`, `item.move`, `item.delete`, `day.moveItems`, `day.clearItems`: `editor` in `OPERATIONS`, so Organizers and Travelers; a Follower and a stranger are told it is not there), through the same guarded transaction as the stops: the trip's counter advances once and stamps what was written, `WAYMARK_LIMIT_DAY_ITEMS` is used, and a refused change keeps nothing. **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1295 tests, on each): the item rules (6 tests), every operation (now 17) as a stranger and as a Traveler and a Follower, the sealed fields, a place or a title or both, every refusal, a place by id (a person's or another trip's is not found), the order and the database's order, the limit, the counter, partial updates and the whole check, a place replaced and swept, a move that keeps every field (compared field by field, into another stop's day), a full day, a shrink refused and then accepted after moving or clearing, a removed stop's items and places, deleting the trip, and a person's deletion keeping what they planned; with the sweep taken out of `deleteItem` one test fails. **Left out:** the **actions and mutations** (`src/actions/`, a trip stream, applied by 6.6) are not made: items are online operations until 6.6, as this task says, so nothing is on a device yet and a conflict between two editors is decided by the order the server takes them; items are not in the export (15.3), and no screen draws them (11.3). `before` names an item of the day it is going to, and any other is not found.

#### ✅ 9.4 — Ready and status

**Goal:** planning until ready, then dates. **Design:** §10.2. **Depends on:** 9.2.

**Technical:** `trip.ready_at`; mark ready (Organizers; needs a dated stop) and back to planning; status computed in `src/trips/status.ts`, pure, in the
first stop's zone; "these dates have passed" for a planning trip in the past; `trip_ready` raised once 12.1 is in.

**Accepts when:** fixtures give each status at the day's edges in the stop's zone; a trip with no dated stop cannot be marked ready.

**Done as:** **`src/trips/status.ts`** (pure: it reads no database and no clock): `statusOf({ readyAt, startDate, endDate }, now, zone)` gives Planning, Upcoming, Ongoing or Completed and `datesPassed`, deciding **today as the date in the first dated stop's zone** (`localDate`, UTC where that stop has no zone), so a trip that begins in Auckland begins at Auckland's midnight whoever asks. **A trip that is ready and has lost its dates** (its last dated stop was removed) is Planning, since nothing decides it. `ready_at` is the only stored field; every `TripView` carries `status` and `datesPassed`, the zone found by a scalar subquery on the first dated stop so that a list needs no join. **`trip.ready` and `trip.planning`** (Organizers: `organizer` in `OPERATIONS`; others are told it is not there): one statement marks a trip ready **only if the caller is an Organizer, the trip is not ready and it has a stop with dates**, or moves it back only if it is ready; a trip already as asked is left unchanged, with no new notice and no counter advanced, and a trip with no dated stop is refused in a sentence (`invalid`). **Notices** (`src/trips/notify.ts`, raised in the same transaction, for every other active member and never the actor, a member who left, or a stranger): **`trip_ready`** (when it is marked ready and when it goes back to planning) and **`trip_dates`** (when the dates of a **ready** trip change, once for the change, in the plan's transaction, also when it loses them); each says the trip's name, its dates and a link, and nothing of a place, note or person; both new kinds are in Settings, the bell and core's bell by default and not mail. **A change to the design:** `trip_dates` is for a _ready_ trip, where §17.1 said every trip, since while a trip is planned its dates change with every stop and the bell would be nothing but that; `DESIGN.md` §17.1 now says so. **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1324 tests, on each, and the status and date tests again under three machine time zones (`pnpm test:zones`)): fixtures give each status at the day's edges in Auckland and in Lisbon, on a single-day trip, on the night a clock changes and in the fallback zone (8 tests); a trip with no dated stop cannot be marked ready and nothing changes or is told; the dates having passed, and then marking ready; back to planning and asking twice; only an Organizer (a Traveler, a Follower, a former member and a stranger are told it is not there); a ready trip that loses its dates; who is told and who is not, what a notice says and that it is sealed; a refused change raising nothing; and the dates notice not raised while planning or when the dates stay; with the dated-stop condition taken out one test fails. **Left out:** `trip_ready` and `trip_dates` are not yet sent as a push (phase 3) and the trip's page that their link opens is 11.2; Trip Mode and the reminders, which need Ongoing, are 13.x; the notification of the dates is raised for each change, not once for a burst of them.

#### ✅ 9.5 — Attachments

**Goal:** files on a trip or a day. **Design:** §10.6. **Depends on:** 9.1, 3.2.

**Technical:** `attachment` (title sealed) through 3.2 (`media.trip_id`, PDF or image, 15 MB); kind; on the trip or a day; upload, rename, move, delete;
Organizers and Travelers only, in every statement and in `/api/media`'s check; deleted with the trip.

**Accepts when:** a Follower gets not found for an attachment's row and its file; deleting the trip deletes its files.

**Done as:** **`attachment`** on both dialects (migration `0019_attachments`; the design's columns and **`thumb_id`**, an image's thumbnail, which `DESIGN.md` §24.3 now lists, since the thumbnail's id is a hash of the file's and so cannot be found from it): the title is sealed (`SEALED.attachment`, in the key rotation with a `seq` guard), the kind is one of receipt, booking, accommodation, ticket and other, and it is on the trip (`day_id` null) or on one of its days. **The media store has an owner** (`src/media/service.ts`: `Owner` is an account or a trip, exactly one, as a `place` is) and `putTripMedia` stores a trip's file judged by its bytes as any other (a PDF as it is, an image re-encoded with a thumbnail, up to 15 MB), sealed on disk under its own data key; **a trip's file is counted against no one's room** (it has no account: the trip's own limit, `WAYMARK_LIMIT_TRIP_ATTACHMENTS`, now used, counts attachments), and a person's own file routes never find one (`account_id = ?` does not match a row with none). **`src/trips/attachments.ts`:** `addAttachment`, `listAttachments`, `updateAttachment` (title, kind, moved between a day and the trip), `deleteAttachment`, `readAttachmentFile` and `readFileFor`. **The permission is in every statement and it is `ATTACHMENT_READERS` / `EDITORS` (Organizers and Travelers), never Followers:** a Follower, a former member and a stranger get no list, no row and no file or thumbnail, and are told only what they would be told if there were none. An add checks the caller before the file is stored, stores it, and writes the row in the transaction whose first statement is the guarded `UPDATE trip` that carries the permission again, so **a caller lowered meanwhile adds nothing and the file is removed** (tested by taking the role away as the store makes its row); a refused or failed add removes the file it stored. **`POST /api/trips/<id>/attachments`** (the body is the file; **the title is in a header, `X-Attachment-Title`, never in the address, which is logged**; `?kind=` and `?day=`; `accountRoute`, so session and `Origin` come first; the same 60-an-account-a-minute allowance as the other uploads) answers 201 with the attachment, **404 with nothing for a Follower, a former member and a stranger alike**, 400 for a value that is not an attachment's, 413 for a file too big or a trip with as many as allowed, 415 and 422 for a file that is not a PDF or an image or cannot be read, each in a sentence that repeats nothing. **`GET /api/media/<id>`** now serves the person's own file, and failing that **a file of an attachment, through one statement that joins it to the attachment and to the caller's membership as an Organizer or Traveler** (so for a Follower the file and its thumbnail are 404), with the same headers as any file (`nosniff`, `Cache-Control: private`, a PDF inline under a sandboxing policy, never as HTML). Rename, move and delete and the list are operations (`attachment.list`, `attachment.update`, `attachment.delete`: `editor` in `OPERATIONS`, so the role test calls each as a stranger and as a Traveler and a Follower). **What becomes of the files:** **deleting the trip removes its attachment and media rows in the transaction and its files after it** (`removeTripRows` returns their ids; a crash between leaves files with no row, which the orphan sweep takes, tested); a day that goes (a stop removed, a stop shrunk) **hands its attachments to the trip**, so a file is never lost with a day; an account's deletion clears `created_by` and **keeps what they attached, as the trip's**, and deletes the files of a trip that went with its last member (tested with the real `deletePerson`). **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1380 tests, on each): a PDF kept as it is and an image re-encoded with a thumbnail, the file an envelope on disk and the title sealed, files judged by bytes (an HTML page named `.pdf`, an empty file, one too big) with the store's own sentence and nothing kept, titles, kinds and days that are not an attachment's, the limit, every role on every statement and on the upload route and the file route, a Follower's thumbnail, the person's own route never finding a trip's file (and a trip never serving a person's), no effect on anyone's room, rename and move and delete, a day's removal, the trip's deletion and the sweep, and an account's; **with the Follower-reads-the-file condition put in, one test fails.** **Left out:** the key rotation moves the files by the directory (all of them, a trip's included: its `attachment` titles are in the row phases) and was not given a trip-specific test; attachments are not in the export (15.3); there is no screen (11.6) and no way to upload from a device with no connection (online only, §15.7); a PDF is served inline under the sandboxing policy and has not been opened in a browser here; and a rename of a title is the only edit of a file's metadata (a file is replaced by deleting it and adding another).

#### ✅ 9.6 — Links

**Goal:** a trip's links, Tally first. **Design:** §10.7. **Depends on:** 9.1.

**Technical:** `trip_link` (title and address sealed), up to 10, `https` only; kind `tally` when chosen as _Expenses in Tally_; never fetched, no preview;
add, edit, reorder, remove.

**Accepts when:** an `http:` or `javascript:` address is refused, and no request is ever made to a link's address.

**Done as:** **`trip_link`** on both dialects (migration `0020_trip_links`): the kind (`tally` when chosen as _Expenses in Tally_, else `other`) and the order are plain, **the title and the address are sealed** (`SEALED.trip_link`: an address can carry a token in its path or query), and it joins the key rotation with a `seq` guard. **`src/trips/link-format.ts`** (pure): **an `https://` address with a host and nothing else**: `http:`, `javascript:`, `data:`, `file:`, `blob:`, `mailto:`, a scheme-relative `//host`, a bare `example.org`, the parser's lenient `https:example.org`, an address with a name or password in it, with a space or control character, empty or over 2,000 characters are all refused, and a sentence says what is wrong without repeating it; the address is kept as the URL parser writes it, so two spellings are one; a title is one line of 1 to 120 characters, and **a Tally link left without a title is called "Expenses in Tally"**. **`src/trips/links.ts`:** `listLinks` (**every member, Followers too**), `addLink` (at the end or before another), `updateLink` (the fields given change and **the whole is checked as it will be**), `moveLink` and `removeLink` (Organizers and Travelers, the permission in a guarded `UPDATE trip` that starts each change and stamps what it writes with the trip's counter; a Follower, a former member and a stranger are told the trip is not there); **at most ten**, and past ten a sentence says so and nothing is made; the order is a fractional position the database sorts the same way as the code (`COLLATE "C"` on PostgreSQL, read back in a test); a link or neighbour of another trip is not found. Operations `link.list` (`member`), `link.add`, `link.update`, `link.move`, `link.remove` (`editor`) in `OPERATIONS`, so the role test calls each as a stranger and as a Traveler and a Follower (it now covers 29). A trip's deletion removes its links; they have no account to clear. **No request is ever made to a link's address**, and two tests say so: **every operation is run with `fetch` replaced by one that counts and refuses every request that is not to this machine (the database itself, on sqld, is one), and none is made**; and **the link files are read for the calls they must not make** (`fetch`, `node:http`, `undici`, an outbound helper, a name of any app's host). Waymark knows nothing of Tally beyond the kind a person chose. **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1418 tests, on each): the address rules over eighteen refused spellings and the accepted ones, kinds and titles, the seal, the limit of ten and a removal making room, every role on every operation, edits as a whole with a refused one leaving the link as it was, ordering, another trip's links never reached, and deletion with the trip; with `http:` allowed four tests fail across the two files, and with a `fetch` of the address added two fail on each database form. **Left out:** the link is not in the export (15.3) and has no screen (11.2 shows it and offers to open it, which is the browser's own request, made when the person chooses); Waymark does not recognise a Tally address (the person chooses the kind), as the design says it knows nothing of Tally.

#### ✅ 9.7 — Auto-link

**Goal:** check-ins fall into the trip their date and place match. **Design:** §12. **Depends on:** 9.2, 6.6, 5.2.

**Technical:** `src/autolink/`, pure: candidates by local date among trips where the person goes, the nearest stop that day, the shorter trip, the
earlier made; run in the action on the device and again in apply on the server; `link_source` manual never touched; re-decided when a check-in's moment or
place changes; a job re-links a person's automatic links when a trip's stops change dates; Change and Unlink on the check-in; earlier imports re-linked once.

**Accepts when:** fixtures hold for a work trip and a weekend sharing dates, a travel day, a check-in across the date line, and a manual link surviving a

**Done as:** **`src/autolink/decide.ts`** (pure, no database and no clock, and **independent of the order the trips are given in**): `decide(facts, trips)` and `reconcile(link, decided)`. **Candidates** are the trips the person goes on (a Traveler, or a going Organizer) whose dates contain the check-in's **local date, its moment in its own zone** (23:30 on the 10th in Auckland is the 10th whatever UTC says; the same instant in Honolulu is the 9th); a trip still planning counts if it has dates; **the nearest stop on that date wins** (a travel day belongs to both stops, so both are measured, and only stops that are on the date are); **where distance does not decide** (no point, or the nearest stops within a kilometre of each other, `NEAR_METRES`) **the shorter trip, then the one made first, then the lesser id**; no candidate, no link. **`reconcile` never changes a manual link**, to a trip or to none. **`src/autolink/server.ts`** gathers what `decide` is given for one account, in statements that name the person's own membership and role (nothing of another's trips is read, nothing sealed is in a condition: the stops' points are opened after), and `mayLink`, Organizer or Traveler. **In `src/sync/apply.ts`, with every check-in the one write path applies:** a link set or removed by hand (`manual`) is **kept as sent after checking that the person is an Organizer or a Traveler of that trip** (a Follower, a former member, a stranger and a trip that is not there are all refused as `link`, the mutation going to Couldn't sync and nothing written), and any other is **decided again by the server with the same function**, so a device that says a check-in is in a trip it is not in, or a stale one, is corrected and the two always agree; this is also how **an import links every imported check-in** (`applyBatch` sends none), and **an edit of the moment or the place re-decides an automatic link** and not a manual one. **`src/autolink/queue.ts` and the `relink-trips` job (every 15 s):** a trip is **asked for in the transaction of the change** (migration `0021_relink`; a stop whose dates change, one added or removed with dates, and a role or "going" change, never a refused change, a note or an undated stop) and **a job claims it by a lease in the database** (two workers or processes never work one; tested at the same moment) and works it a batch of 100 at a time, each batch a short transaction: every check-in **now linked to the trip by auto-link** (whoever's, whatever became of their part in it) and, for each person who goes, their **unlinked or automatically linked** check-ins in its dates (a day each side, for zones). Changes are written **in each owner's own stream** (the account's counter advanced once for all of theirs, each row a new version and a place in the order, so their devices pull it), and only if the row is still as it was read and **still not by hand**. A newly dated trip picks up earlier check-ins this way; the row is deleted only if the trip was not asked for again meanwhile; asking again keeps one row; a trip that is gone is done and forgotten; deleting a trip takes its request with it. **Seen**, on a `file:` database, sqld and PostgreSQL (the whole suite, 1448 tests, on each) and the pure function under three machine time zones (`pnpm test:zones`): the fixtures of the design (a work trip and a weekend that share dates, in both orders; a travel day; across the date line and at 23:59; where distance does not decide and the ties, in any order of trips; the kilometre), every refusal and every case of a link by hand, the job linking a newly dated trip and leaving what is outside, what is by hand and another person's alone, a re-date moving an automatic link and keeping a manual one, a role change, two workers, a lease, more check-ins than a batch (230), and **a run of 40 random check-ins over random trips in which the server's decision equals the pure function's, decided from the whole of the person's trips as a device would hold them**; with the manual guard of `reconcile` taken out one test fails, and with the permission check on a manual link taken out one fails. **Left out:** **the device's side**: the actions already take an auto-link hook (`ctx.link`) and `decide` is what it will call, but trips are not on the device until **6.6**, so a check-in made offline is linked by the server when it syncs, not at once on the device (the two will agree, and this is tested from the same facts); **Change and Unlink on the check-in** are screens (7.3) over the actions that exist (`patch.trip`); there is no re-linking of what an Organizer's removal of a person (10.4) changes yet, which joins with that task; the job is not yet watched for a trip with a very long history beyond the batching tested, and does not report what it changed to the person.
